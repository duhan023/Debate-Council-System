# Boardroom — roles and reference frameworks

## Hierarchy

```
Chair (CEO)
├── CFO      — cost, cash, payback, ROI, budget fit
├── CTO      — feasibility, build/buy/partner, capability, time-to-working
├── COO      — capacity, sequencing, dependencies, timeline, process load
├── CRO/GC   — legal, regulatory, contractual, reputational, tail risk
├── CHRO     — skills, hiring, incentives, change load, who does the work
│
├── VP owners (one per workstream the decision creates; supply ground-truth effort)
│
└── Advisory bench (non-voting; test arguments against frameworks, cite by name)
    ├── Professor of Strategy
    ├── Professor of Finance / Economics
    └── Professor of Organisational Behaviour
```

Each officer argues only from their seat. The CEO weighs; the bench advises; nobody votes as a bloc. A seat with nothing relevant to say says "no objection" in three words and yields.

## What each officer must bring

| Officer | Must state | Must not do |
|---|---|---|
| CFO | A cost, a cash impact, a payback or ROI, and whether it fits a stated ceiling | Say "expensive" without a number |
| CTO | What has to exist for this to work, whether it exists, and time-to-working | Assume capability the user hasn't described |
| COO | Who does the work, in what order, by when, and what it displaces | Give a timeline without naming the dependency that drives it |
| CRO/GC | The specific clause, rule, or exposure, and its consequence | Raise "compliance" generically |
| CHRO | Skills gap, hiring lead time, incentive alignment, change fatigue | Treat people as fungible |
| VP owner | Effort in hours or weeks for their workstream, and the single hardest part | Sandbag or round down |

## Frameworks the advisory bench may cite

Cite by name and apply correctly. If unsure a framework says what you think it says, search before citing. These are starting points, not a closed list.

**Strategy**
- Porter — Five Forces; generic strategies (cost / differentiation / focus)
- Rumelt — *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy*: diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action
- Christensen — disruption theory; jobs-to-be-done
- Real options reasoning (Dixit & Pindyck; Trigeorgis) — value of keeping choices open
- Amazon "working backwards" (press release / FAQ first)
- OKRs (Doerr, *Measure What Matters*; Grove, *High Output Management*)

**Finance / economics**
- NPV, IRR, payback period; hurdle rates
- Unit economics — CAC, LTV, contribution margin
- Kahneman & Tversky — loss aversion, sunk-cost fallacy, planning fallacy; reference-class forecasting (Flyvbjerg)
- Expected value under uncertainty; scenario weighting

**Execution / operations**
- PMI PMBOK Guide — scope, schedule, cost, risk processes; RACI
- Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) — find the bottleneck first
- Lean / Toyota Production System — waste, flow, pull
- Critical path method
- Klein — pre-mortem (2007, *Harvard Business Review*)

**Organisation / change**
- Kotter — 8-step change model; short-term wins
- McKinsey 7S — strategy, structure, systems, shared values, skills, style, staff
- Drucker — management by objectives; "what gets measured gets managed"
- Edmondson — psychological safety and execution quality
- Bridges — transitions vs change
- Conway's law (structure shapes output)

**Risk / governance**
- COSO ERM framework
- ISO 31000 risk management
- Taleb — asymmetric downside, ruin risk vs recoverable loss
- Kill criteria / tripwires (Heath & Heath, *Decisive*)

## Rules the bench enforces

1. A framework is cited to test an officer's argument, never to replace it. "Kotter says communicate" is not an argument; "Kotter's step 6 predicts motivation collapses without a visible win inside 90 days, and the plan has none until month 6" is.
2. A framework misapplied or invented is struck, same as a fabricated figure.
3. The bench flags when two frameworks disagree and says which fits this case better and why.
4. The bench never votes. Advice, then silence.
